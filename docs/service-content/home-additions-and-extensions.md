### Addition or extension: what's the difference?

People use the words loosely, so here's how we use them.

An **addition** creates rooms that didn't exist before. A new bedroom off the back of the
house, a primary suite, a second floor.

An **extension** makes a room you already have bigger. Push the kitchen out eight feet and
suddenly there's space for an island. Same room, more of it.

Which one makes sense depends on what's missing. If you need another room, it's an addition.
If you've got the right rooms and they're just too small, it's usually an extension.

### Additions we build

**Room additions.** A new bedroom, family room, playroom or office, built onto the side or back
of the house.

**Primary suite additions.** A bedroom with its own bathroom and closet, so the main bedroom
finally feels like yours.

**Second-story additions.** When the lot's too tight to build out, you can build up. A second
story can add a lot of space without giving up any yard.

**Kitchen and room extensions.** Pushing out an existing wall to make a cramped kitchen,
dining room or living room bigger. These are often called bump-outs.

**In-law suites.** A bedroom, a bathroom and often a small kitchen, so a parent or relative can
live with you and everyone still has some privacy.

**Sunrooms and four-season rooms.** Lots of windows and real insulation and heat, so you're not
only using it in June.

**Garage additions.** A new attached garage, or one with a bedroom or office built over it.

**Dormers.** If your upstairs has sloped ceilings you can't stand under, a dormer raises part of
the roof and turns cramped space into rooms you can use.

**Two-story additions.** Two new floors at once. You get twice the space on the same footprint,
and only one foundation to pay for.

**Bathroom additions.** One bathroom for a busy family is a daily traffic jam. Adding one,
without building a whole suite, fixes that.

**Mudroom and entryway additions.** A small addition at the back or side door gives coats,
shoes, bags and muddy boots somewhere to go that isn't the kitchen floor.

**Porch enclosures.** You already have the roof and the floor. Enclosing the porch with real
walls, windows and insulation turns it into a room you can use year-round.

**Front porch and portico additions.** A covered entry keeps rain off you at the front door and
gives the front of the house a lot more character.

**Accessory dwelling units.** A separate unit with its own entrance, for a relative, a grown
kid or a rental, where your town's zoning allows it.

**Modular and prefab additions.** The addition's built off-site, then set in place and connected
to your house. It can shorten the time your yard's a job site.

### What has to be figured out first

Every addition starts with a few practical questions, and the answers shape everything after.

What's the space for? A bedroom, a kitchen and a family room each need different things, from
plumbing to windows.

Where can it go? Your town's zoning rules control how close you can build to the property line
and how much of the lot you can cover. Some lots have plenty of room. Some don't, and that's
when building up starts to make sense.

What will it sit on? The new space needs a foundation suited to your site, whether that's a
full basement, a crawl space or a slab.

How will it connect? Heat, air conditioning and electricity all need to reach the new space,
and your current systems may or may not be able to handle it.

We'll go through all of this at your free estimate, so you know what you're getting into before
you commit to anything.

### It should look like it was always there

The best additions don't look like additions. The rooflines line up. The siding and windows
match. Inside, the floors carry through and the ceiling heights make sense.

Where the new roof meets the old one is the part that has to be done right. It's the spot most
likely to leak if it isn't. We frame, flash and seal that connection carefully, so the addition
stays as dry as the rest of the house.

### FAQ

**How much does a home addition cost in New Jersey?**
It depends on size, foundation, whether it's one story or two, and whether it needs plumbing.
A small extension and a second-story suite are very different projects. We'll give you a
written price after a free estimate.

**How long does a home addition take?**
It depends on the size of the addition and on permit approval time in your town. You'll get a
schedule with your estimate, and we'll keep you posted as the work moves.

**Does an addition add value to my home?**
Usually, yes, especially when it adds a bedroom or bathroom and fits the rest of the house.
An addition that looks tacked on or doesn't match the neighborhood adds less. That's one more
reason to make it look like it was always there.

**Can I live at home while you build?**
In most cases, yes. Most of the work happens outside the house until we open the wall to
connect the new space. We'll plan that step so it disrupts as little as possible.

**Do you handle permits?**
Yes. We always pull the permits and schedule the inspections, so you don't have to deal with
the township yourself.
