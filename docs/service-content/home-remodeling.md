### What drives the cost

Photo (left of the text): walls opened to the studs mid-remodel, `src/assets/home-remodeling/remodel-walls-opened-up.webp` (illustrative).

The size of the room matters, but it's rarely the biggest factor. These usually are:

- Moving plumbing, gas lines or walls. In a kitchen, keeping the sink, stove and fridge where
  they are is the biggest single way to keep the cost down.
- The cabinets, countertops, tile and fixtures you choose
- What's behind the walls once they're opened up, like old wiring or water damage
- Whether it's one room or several

### FAQ

The detail on individual rooms lives here rather than in its own sections, so the page doesn't
repeat the "What we remodel" cards.

**How much does a kitchen remodel cost in New Jersey?**
It depends mostly on whether the layout changes and which materials you pick. Keeping the
layout and updating finishes costs far less than moving the sink and stove. We'll give you a
written price after a free estimate.

**Should I remodel or move?**
If you like your neighborhood and the house has good bones, remodeling usually gets you what
you want for less than buying and moving. If the house fundamentally doesn't work, it might not.
We'll give you an honest read on which it is.

**What makes a bathroom remodel last?**
Mostly the parts you'll never see: the waterproofing behind the shower walls, the slope of the
floor toward the drain, and a fan that actually pulls the moisture out. Skip those and new tile
looks great for a year or two, until it doesn't. We build those parts properly first, then the
finishes you picked.

**Can you take out a load-bearing wall?**
Usually, yes. It needs a properly sized beam to carry the weight the wall was holding, and in a
townhouse or condo, the association's OK. We'll tell you which walls can come out and what it
takes.

**Do I need to fix water problems before finishing my basement?**
Yes. Basements sit below ground, so moisture is the first thing we check. Any water problem has
to be fixed before framing goes up, or it ends up hidden behind your new walls.
`[CONFIRM: JPR does basement waterproofing or works with someone who does; if so, say so here]`

**Can my attic be turned into a bedroom?**
Often, yes, but it takes more than drywall. It needs a floor strong enough to live on, a proper
stair, insulation and enough headroom. We'll check what your attic can support before you
commit.

**Can I convert my garage into living space?**
In most houses, yes. The floor gets raised and insulated, and the big door opening becomes a
real wall with windows. In a townhouse, the association usually has to approve it first. We'll
look at what yours needs.

**What order does a whole-home renovation happen in?**
Structure and anything inside the walls goes first, then surfaces, then finishes. Planning it
all together means floors run continuously and you're not undoing last year's project to start
this year's. You can stay put and go room by room, or move out and let us move faster.

**Can I stay home during the remodel?**
For most single-room projects, yes. We'll seal off the work area and keep a path clear. For a
whole-home renovation, some families stay and some don't. We'll talk it through with you.

**Do you remodel condos?**
Yes. Every association has its own rules about approvals, work hours and what can change
inside a unit. We'll go over your building's rules with you before we plan anything, and work
within them.

**Do you handle permits?**
Yes. We always pull the permits your remodel needs and schedule the inspections, so you don't
have to deal with the township yourself.
