### Composite or wood?

Both make good decks. It mostly comes down to budget and how much upkeep you want.

| | Composite | Pressure-treated wood |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cost up front** | Higher. | Lower. |
| **Upkeep** | Wash it a couple of times a year. No staining or sealing. | Clean and seal or stain it every couple of years. |
| **Look** | Even color and grain, in a range of colors. | Real wood grain, and you can stain it any color. |
| **Over the years** | Holds its color. Won't rot, crack or splinter. | Can crack, warp and weather without upkeep. |

Either way, most decks sit on a pressure-treated wood frame. That frame is where a deck's
lifespan is really decided.

### The part you won't see

`[PHOTO: framing, footings or ledger during a build]`

Boards are what you notice. What keeps a deck safe is underneath.

**Footings** go below the frost line, so the deck doesn't heave and shift every winter.

**Framing** is sized for the span and the load, so the deck doesn't bounce when people walk
across it.

**The ledger,** where the deck attaches to the house, gets bolted properly and flashed so water
runs away from the house instead of behind the deck. A badly attached ledger is one of the most
common reasons decks fail, and it's one of the first things inspectors look at.

We build to code, pull the permit and schedule the inspections, and we'll show you how your
deck's built before the boards go on.

### Repair or replace?

`[PHOTO: a resurfaced deck, before and after]` (right of the text)

Not every tired deck needs to come down. If the frame and footings are solid, resurfacing it
with new boards, railings or stairs can make it feel new again for a lot less.

Watch for these signs:

- Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot
- Rot at the posts, joists or where the deck meets the house
- Railings that wobble when you lean on them
- Stairs that shift or bounce
- Rusty nails, screws or hangers
- A gap opening up between the deck and the house

The last two can be safety problems, so don't wait on them. We'll come look and tell you
straight whether your deck can be repaired or should be replaced.

### FAQ

Repair-or-replace and composite-vs-wood aren't here, because the page answers them in their own
sections.

**How much does a new deck cost in New Jersey?**
It depends on the size, the material and how high the deck sits. Composite costs more up front
than wood. You'll get a written price after a free estimate.

**Do I need a permit for a deck in New Jersey?**
In most New Jersey towns, yes. We pull the permit and schedule the inspections for you.

**Can you build on a sloped yard?**
Yes. A slope usually means taller posts on the low side, or a multi-level deck that steps down
with the yard. We'll look at your yard and show you the options.

**Do you build in the rain?**
Some steps can go ahead in light rain. Others, like pouring footings, wait for the weather. We'll
keep you posted if weather moves the schedule.
