It comes down to upfront cost versus upkeep. Wood costs less to install and has a classic look, but it needs to be sealed or stained periodically to stay protected. Trex composite costs more up front, but it resists rot, splintering, and fading, and it never needs staining. We'll walk you through both so you can pick what fits your budget and how much maintenance you want.
