## Trex composite vs. wood decking

The right material depends on your budget and how much upkeep you want. We build with both and will walk you through the trade-offs for your yard.

### Trex composite

- Doesn't need staining or sealing
- Resists rot, splintering, and fading
- Holds its color and finish for years
- Costs more up front than wood

### Wood

- Lower upfront cost
- A natural, classic look
- Needs sealing or staining every few years to stay protected
- Can split, warp, or weather over time if it isn't maintained

Many composite decks are built on a pressure-treated wood frame, so the structure underneath matters just as much as the boards you choose.

</Band>

<Band>

## The structure you don't see

The boards are what you walk on, but the structure is what keeps a deck safe. We build that part carefully:

- **Footings** set deep enough to resist frost heave through New Jersey winters
- **Framing** sized for the deck's spans and loads
- **The ledger connection** fastened and flashed where the deck meets your house, so water can't get behind it

That last point is where many decks fail. Water that gets behind the ledger rots the framing and the house wall. Keeping water out is our trade as roofers, and we bring it to every deck.

</Band>

<Band>

## Signs your deck needs repair or replacement

- Soft or spongy boards
- Rot at the posts, joists, or ledger board
- Loose or wobbly railings
- Stairs that shift or bounce underfoot
- Rusted fasteners or connectors
- A deck that's pulling away from the house

If the footings, framing, and ledger are sound, worn boards, railings, and stairs can usually be repaired. If the structure itself is rotted or failing, a replacement is the safer call. We'll inspect your deck and tell you honestly which one you need.

</Band>

## Railings, stairs, and covered decks

**Railings and stairs** take the most day-to-day wear on a deck. We build new railings and stairs and replace worn ones, in wood or composite to match your deck.

**Covered decks** add a roof for shade and rain cover, so you can use the deck in more weather. A deck roof has to be supported by the structure below and tied into your house so water drains away instead of in. That tie-in is roofing work, and it's work we do every day.

</Band>
