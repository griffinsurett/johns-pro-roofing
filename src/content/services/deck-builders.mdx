## The part you won't see

Boards are what you notice. What keeps a deck safe is underneath.

**Footings** go below the frost line, so the deck doesn't heave and shift every winter.

**Framing** is sized for the span and the load, so the deck doesn't bounce when people walk across it.

**The ledger,** where the deck attaches to the house, gets bolted properly and flashed so water runs away from the house instead of behind the deck. A badly attached ledger is one of the most common reasons decks fail, and it's one of the first things inspectors look at.

We build to code, pull the permit and schedule the inspections, and we'll show you how your deck's built before the boards go on.

</Band>

<Band photoNeeded="A resurfaced deck, before and after" reverse>

## Repair or replace?

Not every tired deck needs to come down. If the frame and footings are solid, resurfacing it with new boards, railings or stairs can make it feel new again for a lot less.

Watch for these signs:

- Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot
- Rot at the posts, joists or where the deck meets the house
- Railings that wobble when you lean on them
- Stairs that shift or bounce
- Rusty nails, screws or hangers
- A gap opening up between the deck and the house

The last two can be safety problems, so don't wait on them. We'll come look and tell you straight whether your deck can be repaired or should be replaced.
