## Addition or extension: what's the difference?

People use the words loosely, so here's how we use them.

An **addition** creates rooms that didn't exist before. A new bedroom off the back of the house, a primary suite, a second floor.

An **extension** makes a room you already have bigger. Push the kitchen out eight feet and suddenly there's space for an island. Same room, more of it.

Which one makes sense depends on what's missing. If you need another room, it's an addition. If you've got the right rooms and they're just too small, it's usually an extension.

## What has to be figured out first

Every addition starts with a few practical questions, and the answers shape everything after.

What's the space for? A bedroom, a kitchen and a family room each need different things, from plumbing to windows.

Where can it go? Your town's zoning rules control how close you can build to the property line and how much of the lot you can cover. Some lots have plenty of room. Some don't, and that's when building up starts to make sense.

What will it sit on? The new space needs a foundation suited to your site, whether that's a full basement, a crawl space or a slab.

How will it connect? Heat, air conditioning and electricity all need to reach the new space, and your current systems may or may not be able to handle it.

We'll go through all of this at your free estimate, so you know what you're getting into before you commit to anything.

</Band>

<Band photoNeeded="Finished interior of the new space">

## It should look like it was always there

The best additions don't look like additions. The rooflines line up. The siding and windows match. Inside, the floors carry through and the ceiling heights make sense.

Where the new roof meets the old one is the part that has to be done right. It's the spot most likely to leak if it isn't. We frame, flash and seal that connection carefully, so the addition stays as dry as the rest of the house.

</Band>
