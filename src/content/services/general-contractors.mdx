## Home renovations and improvements

Most renovations touch more than one part of the house. Updating a kitchen can mean new framing, plumbing and electrical changes, and finish work. Bringing an older home up to date can mean new windows, siding, and a new roof at the same time.

The hard part isn't any single trade. It's the order they happen in. When framing isn't done, the next crew waits. When a roof goes on after the siding, work gets torn up and redone. We sequence the job so each stage is ready for the next one.

</Band>

<Band image={bandConstruction} imageAlt="Residential crew at work on a New Jersey home">

## Residential construction

New residential construction starts with the parts you don't see: footings, framing, and a roof and walls that keep water out. Those are the stages where shortcuts cause problems for years, and they're where we put our attention.

Roofing is our trade, so the building envelope gets the same care we give every roof: solid decking, proper flashing, and clean transitions where water would otherwise find its way in.

</Band>

## Why hire one general contractor instead of separate trades?

When you hire trades separately, you become the project manager. You schedule each crew, make sure the last one finished before the next one starts, and sort it out when something doesn't line up.

With a general contractor, one company owns the schedule, the budget, and the result. If something comes up mid-project, there's one call to make, and it's our job to fix it.
